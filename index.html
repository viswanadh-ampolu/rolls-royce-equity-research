Business overview

Rolls-Royce designs, manufactures and services engines and power systems for commercial aircraft, armed forces and industry. The investment case rests on one fact: Rolls-Royce earns most of its profit from servicing its installed engine base for decades after delivery, not from selling engines.

Civil Aerospace is over half of revenue, so the company is best compared with aero-engine and aftermarket franchises such as GE Aerospace and Safran, not diversified industrials. Defence and Power Systems add growth that does not depend on the airline cycle.

The widebody engine market is a small oligopoly with very high barriers to entry. Sole-source status on the Airbus A350 and the UK's sovereign submarine reactor work give Rolls-Royce a franchise that is hard to replicate, and it gets stronger as services grow in the mix.

Key figures

Revenue quality

Civil revenue is largely recurring. An engine delivered today earns Long-Term Service Agreement (LTSA) income, paid per Engine Flying Hour (EFH), for 20+ years. Airlines cannot easily switch engine supplier once an aircraft is bought, which gives Rolls-Royce pricing power at contract renewal. Each new delivery adds to a long stream of future service revenue.

As services grew in the mix, earnings became more predictable, which supports a higher valuation multiple. EV/EBIT is now about 25x, above its 3-year average of 22.1x and 5-year average of 20.4x (Alpha Spread, Sep 2026). Before 2023, EBIT was too depressed for the multiple to be meaningful, so the re-rating reflects the market pricing in durable, service-led earnings. The limit is that, unlike software, servicing engines needs labour and parts, so costs rise with volume.

What drove the margin step-up

Group underlying operating margin rose 340bps, from 19.1% in H1 2025 to 22.5% in H1 2026, and operating profit grew 46% to £2.5bn. Management credits structural levers delivered under CEO Tufan Erginbilgiç, who joined in January 2023. The transformation is now in its fourth year, so the gains are not down to one person or one year.

Outlook & guidance

FY2026 guidance was raised to £4.7–4.9bn underlying operating profit and £3.8–4.0bn free cash flow. Management cited strong first-half execution across all divisions (H1 free cash flow of £2.0bn, up 24%) and continued transformation benefits, which outweighed the impact of the Middle East conflict.

Power Systems raised its target for power-generation original-equipment revenue growth to 2030 from 20% to 25% a year because of data-centre demand, and it is adding capacity at Aiken (South Carolina) and Mankato (Minnesota).

Key risks

High

Air-travel shocks

Civil revenue depends on flying hours. COVID showed how fast they can collapse.

Medium

Supply chain

2025 engine deliveries fell 9% to 259, slowing growth in the installed base.

Medium

Margin sustainability

Part of the recent margin gain comes from one-off contract repricing.

Medium

SMR execution

Nuclear new-build carries high cost and timeline risk.
